# Cost and health additions — October 21, 2026

## Delivery costs

Primary consumer documentation checked October 21, 2026:

- DoorDash: https://help.doordash.com/en-us/consumers/article/what-fees-do-i-pay
- Uber Eats: https://help.uber.com/ubereats/restaurants/article/how-do-consumer-fees-work?nodeId=9e028052-ce30-4e57-b1cb-f36b8e0e9d82

Both describe delivery/service fees whose amounts depend on the order, location,
merchant and/or membership. DoorDash documents a July 2026 fee-structure rollout
with regional exceptions; a universal 15% rate is therefore not asserted. Merchant
commission percentages are not used as consumer charges.

The website starts with the user's original 2024 Midwest project research,
referenced to page 9 of the process book. Illinois is the initial selection;
Indiana, Iowa and Kansas are available through the custom location dropdown.
These historical state figures are not presented as current national averages.

| Research location | Restaurant / serving | At home / serving |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Illinois | $20.00 | $3.85 |
| Indiana | $15.00 | $4.50 |
| Iowa | $15.50 | $4.27 |
| Kansas | $12.00 | $3.58 |

Delivery is a clearly labeled, editable extension of that research for both
services, not a measured result from the original study, scraped checkout,
brand ranking or guaranteed saving:

| Input | Starting example |
| --- | --- |
| Home ingredients per serving | $3.85 (Illinois research) |
| Restaurant meal per serving | $20.00 (Illinois research) |
| Service fee per order | $3.00 |
| Delivery fee per order | $3.00 |
| Optional tip per order | $3.00 |
| Additional restaurant tax | 0% |
| Servings per order | 1 |
| Meals replaced | 1 per day |

Restaurant = researched restaurant meal × (1 + additional tax rate).
Delivery = restaurant + (service + delivery + tip) / servings.
Difference = selected alternative − home ingredient cost.
Annual difference = difference × selected meal count × 365 days or 52 weeks;
displayed rounded to dollars. The custom frequency dropdown defaults to day.
Default: $20.00 restaurant, $29.00 modeled delivery, $25.15 saved versus delivery
per meal, $9,180 rounded annual savings for 365 replaced meals. Versus restaurant,
the original Illinois difference is $16.15 per meal and $5,895 per year at one
meal daily. Negative differences are labeled as extra home-cooking costs.
The original research does not itemize tax, so no extra tax is added by default;
an editable additional rate applies to the restaurant meal only. Utilities,
equipment, travel, subscriptions, promotions and extra local fees are excluded.
Actual taxes, menu prices and checkout fees may differ. Inputs should reflect the
reader's own costs. Editing base costs is marked on the chart. Changing location
restores its researched home/restaurant figures and zero additional tax while
preserving edited fees and servings. Reset restores all assumptions for the
selected state. The full original Midwest table remains available below.

Custom dropdowns use a sliding panel, selected checkmarks and keyboard navigation
(arrows, Home/End, type-ahead, Enter/Space, Escape, Tab), with outside-click dismissal
and reduced-motion support. The same control is used for state, meal count,
day/week and restaurant/delivery comparisons.

## Health evidence

Wolfson JA, Bleich SN. *Is cooking at home associated with better diet quality or
weight-loss intention?* Public Health Nutrition. 2015;18(8):1397–1406.
Published online November 17, 2014. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980014001943

Cross-sectional NHANES 2007–2010 data; final sample 9,569 US adults aged 20+.
Adjusted predicted daily intakes for dinner cooked by someone in the household
0–1 versus 6–7 times per week, from the article's results/Table 3:

| Measure | 0–1×/week | 6–7×/week |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy (kJ/day) | 9,627 | 9,054 |
| Total sugar (g/day) | 135 | 119 |
| Fibre (g/day) | 15.9 | 16.9 |

These describe whole-day consumption, not a particular meal, delivery platform
or MySpice trial. Total sugar is not the same as added sugar. Observational
associations do not establish cause and effect. The page states these limits.

CDC: *Tips for Reducing Sodium Intake*, reviewed/updated July 30, 2026.
https://www.cdc.gov/salt/reduce-sodium-intake/index.html

Used for ingredient-label comparisons, vegetables/fruit/basic ingredients,
limiting ready-made sauces/mixes, and using herbs, spices and citrus in place of
some salt. No treatment, disease-prevention guarantee or measured MySpice health
benefit is claimed.
